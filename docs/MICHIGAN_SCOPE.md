# Michigan mushroom scope for MycoScout 0.4

Reviewed **October 26, 2026**. The machine-readable source is `config/michigan-mushrooms-v1.json`. It covers all **20 entries** in MAMI's current certification study-guide index, 35 primary named species, the explicitly open genus groups, related names, restrictions and unresolved questions. These entries are not uniformly species or genera. They are documented curriculum coverage, **not an exhaustive statutory whitelist**, a claim that an entire genus is edible, or permission generated by the software. [MAMI study-guide index](https://midwestmycology.org/mushroom-study-guides/)

## Certification and inspection

MDARD's May 5, 2026 guidance says a seller must be certified or have the mushrooms inspected by a certified expert. Each individual mushroom must be inspected; stores and restaurants must purchase from an approved source. Experts must be prepared to show their certification. Recognition can follow MAMI training or a comparable MDARD-approved course. A reviewed photograph, user-entered species name, confidence score or model prediction does not satisfy that inspection requirement. [MDARD's current sales guidance](https://www.michigan.gov/mdard/about/media/pressreleases/2026/05/05/mdard-provides-safety-and-sales-guidelines-for-wild-foraged-mushroom-season)

The full MAMI program requires an examination score of at least 80%; certification lasts five years. The curriculum index mixes named species and broader entries. The separate virtual morel program is **morel-only**, also with an examination and five-year certification. Holding that narrower certificate does not extend its scope to chanterelles, oysters or the other groups. The app keeps actual credential scope and expiry separate from research review. [Full program](https://midwestmycology.org/workshops/), [morel-only program](https://midwestmycology.org/)

The currently linked Michigan Modified Food Code, section 3-201.16, contains limited exceptions for regulated cultivation operations and packaged products from regulated food-processing plants. Those provisions do not create a general exemption for a forager or for AI identification. The section does not enumerate a complete species list. [MDARD's Food Code, section 3-201.16](https://www.michigan.gov/mdard/-/media/Project/Websites/mdard/documents/food-dairy/laws/mi_modified_2009_food_code.pdf?hash=A3B937356FF22A06673F7AEBE0E9CFC3&rev=3dea24829f1a4f1c932f9bf1dec2a1e3)

It does not replace an expert's determination of an individual specimen or that expert's actual certification scope. Names are preserved as the source uses them; historical names are not silently treated as current taxonomic synonyms.

The catalog also records **boletes** as research coverage outside this confirmed 20-guide index. Their absence from the index is not evidence of a universal legal ban. A specific sales-scope determination remains unresolved. The app should still accept human-reviewed bolete observations and show that coverage gap.

## Exclusions and uncertain names

The strongest explicit form restriction is the normal, unaborted form of *Entoloma abortivum*. An iNaturalist species label alone cannot distinguish permitted aborted material from that excluded form. [MAMI's form restriction](https://midwestmycology.org/entoloma-abortivum/)

MAMI explicitly excludes *Verpa* from collection and sale and discusses *Gyromitra esculenta, G. brunnea* and *G. korfii* as dangerous morel lookalikes. They are research comparison labels, never positive curriculum targets. [MAMI morel comparisons](https://midwestmycology.org/morchella-americana/morchella-look-alikes/)

Related or historic names retain typed statuses. For example, *M. esculentoides* is a source-reported synonym, while older applications of *M. esculenta* or *M. semilibera* are recorded as historical usage rather than present-day species equivalence. The guide also notes that *M. americana* and *M. cryptica* may not be separable macroscopically. [MAMI morel guide](https://midwestmycology.org/morchella-americana/)

The earlier black-trumpet image corpus includes *C. calicornucopioides* as a global visual research target; that does not establish Michigan certification coverage. Current accessible MAMI pages preserve older taxonomy and open questions. An API taxon match must retain independent provenance and must not silently widen sales scope.

## Sales records and handling

The MDARD training document requires sale records for **at least two years**, available for review. Its fields include quantity by species; scientific and common names; buyer name/contact; identifier name/contact and qualification when the identifier differs from the seller; and a statement that identification occurred while fresh. Retail bulk containers need common and scientific names. Packaged and wholesale products have additional labeling provisions. [MDARD regulations, PDF pages 13–15](https://www.michigan.gov/mdard/-/media/Project/Websites/mdard/documents/food-dairy/mushroom/Michigan-Wild-Foraged-Mushroom-Regulations.pdf)

MAMI separately summarizes two-year records, contamination protection and the distinction between whole mushrooms and processing activities. Its older statement that all drying needs a licensed kitchen conflicts with current MDARD guidance, which permits qualifying raw or dried mushrooms through cottage-food rules with proof of expert identification. Use the regulator's current conditions for the actual operation; the app does not issue business clearance. [MAMI sales summary](https://midwestmycology.org/selling-mushrooms/), [current MDARD cottage-food guidance](https://www.michigan.gov/mdard/food-dairy/cottage-food/what-can-i-make)

Michigan DNR says food harvested from **state lands** is for personal use, not resale, and private-land foraging requires landowner permission. Preserve the land jurisdiction rather than assuming every public parcel has identical rules. [DNR foraging rules](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/things-to-do/foraging)

## Software contract and remaining work

Canonical `groupId` values are `morels`, `chanterelles_trumpets`, `gilled`, `puffballs`, `tooth_fungi`, `polypores`, `crust_parasitized`, `truffles`, `boletes`, and `unknown`. They are practical visual categories, not claims of evolutionary relationship or edibility. Model classes can be more specific, but must map back to these IDs. The two parasitized forms have explicit subtype entries; a group prediction cannot establish which form was photographed.

Each entry exposes `coverageStatus`, `taxa`, `relatedTaxa`, `constraints`, `reviewChecklist`, `researchGaps`, `certificationScopeIds` and `sourceIds`. Every primary source has a retrieval date and URL. These are dated research references, not immutable copies of the live websites. No certificate-holder registry personal data was collected or incorporated.

Human review may retain an uncertain species, alternative names and confidence without upgrading it to a confirmed identification. Research annotations, model guesses and certified fresh-specimen inspection are different records. Exact current taxonomy, actual credential coverage, unresolved genus-level boundaries, independent specimen labels and measured field performance remain separate validation tasks.
